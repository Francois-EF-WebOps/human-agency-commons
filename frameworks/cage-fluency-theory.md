# Cage Fluency Theory — Working Framework (v0.1)

**Status:** Working synthesis; conceptual framework, not a validated scientific theory.  
**Purpose:** A practical, testable vocabulary for preserving useful agency and navigation under changing constraints, imperfect information, finite resources, and time.

## 1. Core definition

**Cage Fluency** is the ability to preserve navigation capability while operating inside, through, and—where feasible—outside evolving constraint systems.

A **constraint system** is any combination of rules, incentives, interfaces, institutions, physical limits, and social expectations that shapes available actions. Constraints are not inherently harmful: some protect people, coordinate action, and enable reliable work.

This framework should be compared with established work in cybernetics, ecological psychology, decision science, cognitive ergonomics, resilience engineering, human factors, and robotics. It must not be presented as empirically validated or wholly novel until reviewed against prior literature and tested.

## 2. The navigation problem

> How does an agent preserve useful navigation capability while operating with imperfect maps, shifting labels, scarce resources, and continuous drift over time?

An agent may be a person, family, team, organisation, or autonomous system. The analogy does not imply that all agents have the same needs, rights, cognition, or moral status.

### Working vocabulary

- **Agent:** A system that observes conditions, selects actions, and produces effects in pursuit of objectives.
- **Environment:** External conditions and other agents that affect available actions and outcomes.
- **Objective:** A desired outcome, including boundaries that must not be violated.
- **Map / projection:** The agent's current representation of relevant reality; partial, uncertain, and purpose-dependent.
- **Navigation capability:** Ability to orient, choose, act, detect consequential error, and revise course while retaining viable options.
- **Constraint system / cage:** Interacting rules, structures, incentives, interfaces, and limits that shape possible actions.
- **Drift:** The accumulation of differences between reality and the agent's current projection of reality.
- **Reconnaissance / scouting:** Purposeful information gathering to resolve a decision-relevant uncertainty.
- **Adaptation:** Changing a map, strategy, skill, resource allocation, or objective in response to relevant evidence.
- **Adaptive continuity:** Maintaining essential function and the capacity to change course without requiring an unchanged plan.
- **Resource budget:** Time, energy, attention, money, trust, health, skills, tools, and relationships available for a task.
- **Babar (archive metaphor):** A working name for a backlog of observations, experiences, and unresolved questions awaiting triage. It is not a diagnosis, and memories are not presumed accurate merely because they feel vivid.
- **Closure:** Resolving, retiring, or deliberately parking an item with a reason and a route to reopen it if new evidence appears.
- **Adaptation score:** A proposed measure that must be operationally defined for each setting; it is not a validated universal score.

## 3. The operating loop

Use this as a revisable decision cycle, not as a claim that all thought follows one sequence.

1. **Orient:** State the objective, boundaries, and current conditions.
2. **Observe:** Collect information relevant to a specific decision.
3. **Compare:** Identify differences between expected and observed outcomes.
4. **Classify:** Separate direct observations, records, interpretations, hypotheses, and unknowns.
5. **Estimate:** Judge uncertainty, consequence, reversibility, and the cost of more information.
6. **Choose:** Select a proportionate action, including pausing, seeking help, or doing nothing.
7. **Act:** Prefer a bounded, reversible step when uncertainty is high.
8. **Review:** Check the result against a predefined signal; update the map and next action.
9. **Close or continue:** Record the reusable lesson, retire the item, or keep it open with an owner and review condition.

This resembles established feedback and decision cycles, including cybernetic control and OODA-style models. This is a comparison to investigate, not proof that the models are interchangeable.

## 4. Drift: types and handling

Drift is not always dangerous. Some differences are harmless, expected, or useful signals that the model needs refinement.

| Drift type | Example | Useful response |
|---|---|---|
| Environmental | Prices, rules, systems, or conditions change | Verify the current source and date |
| Model | An expectation repeatedly fails | Update assumptions and test alternatives |
| Label / semantic | A word is used differently across contexts | Record local meaning, source, and scope |
| Operational | A process no longer produces its intended result | Inspect recent changes and dependencies |
| Resource | Time, energy, funds, or capacity decline | Reduce scope and protect essential functions |
| Objective | The desired outcome changes | Explicitly confirm or revise the objective |
| Social / organisational | Incentives or decision rights shift | Document observable actions and verify independently |

### Drift triage

Ask:
- Does the difference affect a real decision or safety boundary?
- What is directly observed, and what is inferred?
- What independent evidence could distinguish competing explanations?
- What is the cost of being wrong?
- Is a small test cheaper than more analysis?
- When should this be checked again?

Do not infer coordination, intent, targeting, or causation from coincidence or pattern alone. Such explanations require evidence that distinguishes them from alternatives.

## 5. Control, influence, and uncertainty

Use three practical zones:

- **Control:** Actions the agent can directly take, such as drafting an application or setting a review time.
- **Influence:** Outcomes the agent can affect but cannot determine, such as a recruiter response or team decision.
- **Outside current control:** Conditions the agent cannot presently change, such as another person's private motives or a policy already in force.

These zones are context-dependent. Ask: *What is the next ethical, affordable action available to me?*

For social adversity, record concrete behaviour, date, context, impact, and corroboration separately from interpretation. Use proportionate boundaries and ordinary reporting or support channels where warranted. Do not spend scarce resources trying to prove an untestable theory about another person's hidden intent.

## 6. Time, energy, and cognitive load

Time management is necessary but insufficient. A plan can use time efficiently while pursuing the wrong objective or exhausting the person who must continue it.

**Resource allocation** asks:
- What must be protected?
- What action has the highest expected value for the current objective?
- What is its full cost, including recovery and switching?
- What can be paused, delegated, simplified, or retired?
- What evidence is enough to stop investigating?

### Single-thread rule

1. Name the current objective in one sentence.
2. Select one next physical or digital action that can be completed in 15–45 minutes.
3. Park adjacent ideas in a capture list without developing them.
4. Define a stop condition and a return condition.
5. Review the result before opening another workstream.

Multitasking can hide task-switching costs. The goal is not to suppress curiosity, but to prevent every new signal from becoming a new commitment.

Caffeine or urgency may temporarily change alertness, but neither should be treated as a substitute for sleep, food, hydration, medical care when needed, or sustainable workload design. Track actual function and recovery rather than assuming high activity equals high capacity.

## 7. Reconnaissance without endless scanning

Scouting is useful when information can change a decision. It becomes wasteful when observation has no question, budget, or stopping rule.

Before reconnaissance, write:
- **Decision:** What choice will this inform?
- **Uncertainty:** What do I not know?
- **Evidence:** What source could resolve it?
- **Budget:** How much time and energy may be spent?
- **Stop rule:** What result is sufficient to decide?
- **Expiry:** When might the result become stale?

If no plausible finding would change the action, defer the research.

## 8. Babar archive and memory hygiene

Treat the archive as a backlog, not a command to reconstruct every event.

Recommended item states: **INBOX** (captured, not assessed), **OPEN** (current question), **BLOCKED** (waiting for a named dependency), **READY** (next action defined), **IN PROGRESS** (being worked now), **CLOSED** (resolved or intentionally retired), **REOPENED** (new evidence materially changes the question), **ARCHIVED** (reference only), and **SUPERSEDED** (replaced by a newer linked record).

Use a compact record: item ID, neutral title, date/context if known, observation, interpretation, confidence, source/corroboration, practical impact, next action, status, closure rule.

Avoid recording sensitive details unless necessary. Keep raw notes separate from conclusions. A memory is evidence that an experience is remembered, not automatic proof that every detail or inferred cause is accurate. Do not force certainty where the record is incomplete.

**Closure rule:** Close low-priority items after a brief review if they have no actionable consequence, no current safety relevance, and no new independent evidence. Record why they were closed and what would justify reopening them. Closure is not denial.

## 9. Labels, principles, and rules

- **Label:** A short name for a category; useful but lossy.
- **Observation:** A report of what was seen, heard, recorded, or measured.
- **Interpretation:** A possible explanation of an observation.
- **Hypothesis:** An explanation that could be tested against alternatives.
- **Principle:** A reusable rule of thumb with a stated scope and limitations.
- **Rule:** A requirement or boundary set by an authority, agreement, or system.
- **Metric:** A defined measure, not a substitute for judgement.
- **Evidence:** Information supporting or weakening a claim, assessed for relevance and quality.

Use labels to retrieve information, not replace it. A category is worth keeping if it helps choose an action, communicate clearly, or detect a meaningful difference.

## 10. Measuring navigation capability

Do not create one universal numerical adaptation score without validation. Start with observable, context-specific measures:

- **Objective progress:** Did the action move the stated goal?
- **Calibration:** Did confidence roughly match what the evidence supported?
- **Drift detection:** Were consequential changes noticed in time?
- **Recovery:** How quickly did essential function resume after a setback?
- **Resource sustainability:** Was the action affordable without unacceptable downstream cost?
- **Option preservation:** Did it retain useful alternatives or avoid irreversible harm?
- **Closure quality:** Were items resolved, parked, or reopened for explicit reasons?
- **Transfer:** Could the lesson improve a later, comparable decision?

Define the measure, baseline, observation period, data source, and failure conditions before scoring. Avoid comparing unlike people or environments with a single unvalidated number.

## 11. Worked example: job search under constraints

**Objective:** Obtain income through a suitable role while remaining compliant with local work and immigration rules.

- **Map:** Current CV, evidence of skills, location, legal status, equipment, target roles.
- **Drift risks:** Expired vacancies, changed rules, stale salary claims, assumptions about sponsorship.
- **Reconnaissance:** Verify one live vacancy and its requirements.
- **Action:** Tailor one application or send one targeted recruiter message.
- **Feedback:** Record reply, rejection reason if provided, or no response after a defined interval.
- **Adaptation:** Revise one part of the search based on evidence, not an imagined explanation.
- **Closure:** Close stale leads; preserve useful lessons; keep the income-critical thread ahead of speculative side projects.

This is an example of applying the framework, not a claim that it guarantees employment.

## 12. Cross-domain applications

- **Human life and families:** Preserve choice, care, privacy, continuity, and room to change plans.
- **Organisations:** Detect process drift, make decision rights visible, learn from incidents, and avoid metrics that reward activity over outcomes.
- **SRE / infrastructure:** Compare expected telemetry with actual behaviour, manage alert fatigue, define rollback conditions, and preserve service viability.
- **Robotics / AI agents:** Maintain uncertainty-aware world models, detect distribution shift, budget sensing and computation, preserve safe fallback behaviour, and request human input when confidence or authority is insufficient.
- **Education:** Distinguish a student's actual capability from labels and test whether feedback improves transfer.
- **Research:** Keep claims traceable, separate data from interpretation, and publish null results and limitations.

The analogy must respect domain differences. Human dignity, consent, rights, and lived experience cannot be reduced to system uptime or a control score.

## 13. Evidence discipline and research plan

Cage Fluency is a working synthesis. Adjacent established fields to review—not proof that this framework is validated—include cybernetics and feedback control; ecological psychology and affordances; OODA and naturalistic decision-making; cognitive load and task-switching research; sensemaking and distributed cognition; resilience engineering and safety science; active inference and predictive processing; robotics localisation, mapping, uncertainty, and planning under partial observability; and organisational learning.

For each source, record author, title, year, stable URL/DOI, relevant finding, method, limitation, and the exact concept it informs. Distinguish a source's finding from our interpretation. Do not claim that research supports the entire framework because it supports one component.

### Falsifiability questions

1. Does an explicit drift review improve decisions compared with ordinary planning?
2. Does a defined reconnaissance budget reduce wasted effort without missing important signals?
3. Does a closure protocol reduce repeated low-value review while preserving appropriate reopening?
4. Do resource-aware plans improve sustained progress over a fixed period?
5. Do effects vary by task, context, or agent type?

Use comparison groups or within-person baselines when feasible; define outcomes before testing; publish negative or mixed results.

## 14. Versioning and change control

This is version 0.1: a vocabulary and practice proposal, not a final theory.

When changing a core definition:
1. Record the old wording and proposed wording.
2. State the reason and evidence.
3. Note which examples or measures change.
4. Check for contradictions across the glossary, templates, and README.
5. Keep unresolved disagreements visible rather than silently rewriting history.

## 15. Short form

> **Cage Fluency is the practice of preserving useful navigation capability under evolving constraints. Drift describes the gap that grows between reality and a working map. Sustainable navigation requires purposeful observation, calibrated uncertainty, proportionate action, resource-aware pacing, and explicit closure.**

The test of the framework is not whether it explains every past event. It is whether it helps make better, safer, more economical decisions in the next one.

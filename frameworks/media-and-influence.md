# Media, platforms, and influence

## Scope

This framework covers local press, television, radio, audio, social platforms, messaging services, influencers, advertising, political communication, organisational messaging, and AI-generated or AI-mediated content.

The purpose is not to assume that influence is inherently harmful. Communication can inform, connect, organise, and support people. The questions are about transparency, power, effects, and meaningful choice.

## Questions for assessment

- Who created or funded the message?
- Is persuasion, advertising, sponsorship, or automated generation disclosed?
- Can people distinguish reporting, opinion, promotion, satire, and speculation?
- Are sources, edits, and corrections traceable?
- Are recommendation or engagement incentives visible?
- Is the content exploiting fear, urgency, shame, loneliness, or financial vulnerability?
- Can users control notifications, recommendations, data collection, and exposure?
- Can a person leave or disagree without threats, retaliation, or social coercion?
- What are the likely effects on attention, relationships, finances, health decisions, and participation?
- Are there local-language, low-bandwidth, disability-accessible alternatives?

## Consistent standards

Use the same core questions across commercial media, political groups, religious organisations, employers, influencers, and AI systems. Do not label a belief or community an addiction. Evaluate specific practices and observable harms, while respecting lawful belief, expression, association, and voluntary participation.

## Practical responses

Prefer media literacy, transparent sourcing, user-controlled settings, clear correction channels, community-owned communication, and voluntary attention boundaries. Do not prescribe individual “self-control” as the only response when design incentives, unequal access, or coercive power are part of the problem.

## Measurement and caution

Possible measures include correction rates, source transparency, unwanted exposure, user control, reported pressure, time and money costs, and the ability to disengage. These indicators require careful interpretation; high use or strong commitment alone does not establish harm.

# Behaviour change without harm

## Purpose

Understand what makes an action difficult, then support people to choose changes that fit their priorities and conditions. This framework does not treat compliance as success.

## Learning cycle

1. **Observe:** describe a specific situation without diagnosing people from a distance.
2. **Understand barriers:** consider cost, access, safety, time, care work, infrastructure, social pressure, information, and personal preferences.
3. **Co-design:** let affected people define useful outcomes and acceptable trade-offs.
4. **Experiment:** try a small, voluntary, reversible change where risks are low.
5. **Evaluate:** compare outcomes with a baseline or reasonable alternative; record harms and failures.
6. **Adapt:** keep, change, or stop the approach based on evidence and participant feedback.

## Minimum safeguards

- Participation is voluntary and informed.
- People can decline or leave without penalty.
- Essential services are not withheld to force behaviour.
- Risks, costs, and uncertainty are explained.
- Sensitive information is minimised and protected.
- The intervention does not rely on shame, deception, threats, or manufactured dependency.
- A clear stop condition exists.

## Ask before advising

- Is the desired action possible with the person's current resources?
- Is the problem individual, structural, or both?
- Who defined the desired outcome?
- What would count as a burden or harm?
- What alternative explanations could account for the observed result?
- What would make us stop or change course?

## Evidence discipline

A plausible mechanism is not proof of effectiveness. Report context, participation, duration, outcome measures, unintended effects, and limitations. Do not generalise a result beyond the conditions in which it was observed.

# Human Agency Commons

**Open, evidence-led research and practical solutions for human agency, community resilience, and everyday wellbeing.**

The Commons helps people and communities understand the conditions that shape their choices, identify approaches that have evidence behind them, and test locally workable improvements without sacrificing dignity, consent, privacy, or the ability to refuse.

The first applied pilot is **Cabo Verde**. The methods and repository are intended to be reusable elsewhere.

## Why this exists

People's choices are shaped by more than individual motivation. Income, housing, food, water, transport, healthcare, education, care responsibilities, information environments, institutional power, and access to useful tools can expand or constrain what is realistically possible.

This project connects those conditions to practical responses. It does not assume that every problem is an individual failure, that one institution has all the answers, or that a proposed intervention works merely because it sounds plausible.

## Working principles

1. **Agency, not compliance:** support meaningful choice, informed consent, and the ability to decline, pause, or leave.
2. **Evidence before certainty:** distinguish documented facts, reported claims, interpretations, hypotheses, and unknowns.
3. **Structural and personal factors:** consider both material barriers and individual experience; never use behavioural advice to obscure scarcity or inaccessible services.
4. **Local knowledge matters:** seek local-language sources, local journalism, lived experience, and affected-community perspectives alongside formal research.
5. **No coercion or exploitation:** examine harmful conduct consistently across commercial platforms, influencers, political movements, employers, religious organisations, public institutions, and AI systems.
6. **Test proportionately:** prefer low-cost, reversible experiments with clear outcomes and a plan to stop if harm appears.
7. **Privacy by default:** collect the minimum information needed; do not publish identifying or sensitive personal information without valid, informed permission.
8. **Accessible contributions:** welcome small, phone-friendly contributions, translations, corrections, and respectful disagreement.
9. **No invented evidence:** never fabricate sources, quotations, statistics, local testimony, or results.
10. **Show limitations:** record who may be excluded, what an intervention costs, what it depends on, and what the evidence does not establish.

## A shared process

**Observe → understand barriers → co-design → experiment → evaluate → adapt.**

Use this as a learning cycle, not a rigid programme. Community members should help define the problem, outcomes, safeguards, and acceptable trade-offs.

## Initial pilot: Cabo Verde

The initial challenge areas are working categories, not claims that every island or community experiences them identically:

- Cost of living, income insecurity, and underemployment
- Inter-island transport and access to services
- Water, sanitation, drought, and climate resilience
- Healthcare access and workforce retention
- Affordable housing
- Education-to-work transitions
- Gender inequality and unpaid care
- Food security and import dependence
- Waste and energy systems
- Media, digital platforms, audio, public communication, and influence

Religious participation is not itself classified as addiction or harm. Assess specific, observable practices and outcomes—such as coercion, financial exploitation, isolation, interference with healthcare, or loss of meaningful choice—using the same standards applied to other organisations and systems. Do not infer harm from belief or identity alone.

## Repository map

- `evidence/` — source register, claim ledger, and evidence standards
- `frameworks/` — human agency, behaviour, media, and influence
- `cases/cabo-verde/` — pilot scope and challenge register
- `interventions/` — evidence-backed options and locally testable ideas
- `evaluations/` — outcome measures and harm review
- `templates/` — reusable source, claim, case, and proposal records

## How to contribute

You can help by:
- Adding one credible source with its date, location, language, and limitations
- Verifying or challenging one claim
- Documenting an existing solution, including cost and failure modes
- Translating or improving a public resource
- Identifying missing voices, accessibility barriers, or foreseeable harms
- Proposing a small, reversible intervention with measurable outcomes

Read [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) before submitting work. Do not add personal stories or sensitive details about identifiable people without their informed permission.

## Status

This is an early-stage framework and pilot. The repository does **not** yet claim that the listed challenges have all been locally verified, that any proposed intervention has been tested in Cabo Verde, or that a causal relationship has been established. Claims and interventions must earn their status through traceable evidence and evaluation.

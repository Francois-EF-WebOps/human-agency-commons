# Cabo Verde evidence baseline — first pass

**Snapshot date:** 2026-10-17  
**Status:** Initial desk research; not a comprehensive national assessment and not a ranking of needs.

## What this baseline does

This first pass adds traceable official, institutional and local-language sources to the source register and records bounded claims in the claim ledger. It prioritises explicit limitations and next verification steps. A source being listed is not equivalent to independent validation.

## Initial findings supported by sources

### 1. Labour market: improvement in headline unemployment does not settle job quality

INE's IMC 2024 report reports national unemployment at 8.0% in 2024, down from 10.3% in 2023. The official summary also reports labour underutilisation at 28.4% and youth unemployment (ages 15–24) at 20.1%. These are distinct measures: the broader underutilisation rate must not be described as the unemployment rate. The next step is to extract the full definitions and municipality, age, sex and education tables from the INE report.

Sources: [INE IMC 2024 labour report](https://ine.cv/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/imc-2024-estatisticas-mercado-trabalho.pdf); [Direção Geral do Emprego summary](https://www.dge.gov.cv/2025/06/06/mercado-de-trabalho-em-2024-dados-confirmam-melhorias-significativas-no-emprego/).

### 2. Essential consumption: an island-specific basket signals affordability questions, not a universal family budget

ADECO's March 2026 Essential Consumption Index (ICE), as published by ADECO and reported by Expresso das Ilhas, estimated monthly baskets of about CVE 33,156 in Praia, CVE 34,909 on Sal, and CVE 24,856 on São Vicente for one healthy individual. The basket includes assumed quantities and selected costs; reported housing assumptions differ by location. It must not be treated as the actual expenditure of every person, a family budget, or a complete poverty measure. Read the original calculation reports before drawing policy conclusions.

Sources: [ADECO ICE update](https://adeco.cv/noticia/atualizacao-do-ice-sal-e-sao-vicente-marco-2026/); [Expresso das Ilhas report](https://srv1.expressodasilhas.cv/pais/2026/03/30/custo-de-vida-essencial-supera-salario-minimo-adeco/102127).

### 3. Water access: a substantial reported household constraint requires primary-table verification

Inforpress reported from INE's IMC 2024 that 43.9% of households did not have enough water to meet their needs in 2024, with a higher reported proportion in rural areas. This is important enough to investigate, but the exact question, definition, island breakdown and sampling precision should be verified in the primary INE publication. It does not by itself measure water quality, continuous service or affordability.

Source: [Inforpress report attributing the finding to INE](https://inforpress.cv/pt/article-3127).

### 4. Inter-island connectivity: national analysis and a local Brava report point to a researchable issue

The World Bank's July 2026 update identifies weak and costly domestic air and maritime connectivity as a constraint on market integration and inclusive growth. Separately, Inforpress's retrospective on Brava reports that irregular maritime connections during 2025 affected the supply of essential goods. The local report is evidence of reported incidents and local experience, not proof of a uniform national pattern or that transport alone caused every shortage. Triangulation needs route schedules, cancellations, freight costs, prices, operator/port data and additional local accounts.

Sources: [World Bank Economic Update 2026 summary](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/07/14/cabo-verde-economic-update-2026-connecting-islands-unlocking-potential); [Inforpress Brava retrospective](https://www.inforpress.cv/pt/retrospectiva-brava-ilha-marcada-por-defice-de-transporte-maritimo-e-escassez-de-bens-essenciais).

### 5. Food, water, energy and climate form a connected risk area

The World Bank's 2025 climate-report summary states that imports account for 80% of domestic food consumption and highlights drought, imported fuel and goods, and climate exposure. The IMF climate diagnostic also describes the connection between water scarcity, energy-intensive desalination, agriculture and exposure to international price shocks. Before using the 80% figure in a headline, verify the technical report's definition, reference year and commodity coverage. These national-level analyses do not tell us how each household or island is affected.

Sources: [World Bank climate report summary](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/01/15/ambitious-climate-action-will-reap-large-dividends-for-cabo-verde); [IMF climate policy diagnostic](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/019/2024/040/article-A001-en.xml).

### 6. Inflation data and lived affordability are related but not interchangeable

INE's July 2026 CPI release provides official national inflation measures. National CPI measures price changes in a defined basket; they do not by themselves establish what a particular household can afford, the level of rent on every island, or the adequacy of a minimum wage. The analysis should keep CPI, the ADECO ICE, wages and household income/expenditure data distinct, then compare them carefully.

Source: [INE CPI release, July 2026](https://www.ine.cv/divulgacao-detalhe/notas-de-imprensa/4556/indice-de-precos-no-consumidor-nacional-julho-de-2026).

## Important gaps

This first source set does **not** yet support a national ranking or sufficiently specific claims about:
- Healthcare access, waiting times, workforce retention and service quality
- Housing availability, rent distributions, overcrowding and tenure
- Education-to-work transitions and vocational outcomes
- Unpaid care, gendered time use and access to care services
- Current waste-system performance and island-level energy reliability
- Media trust, information access, platform practices or harms
- Harmful conduct by religious, commercial, political or other organisations

These are evidence gaps in this repository's first pass, not proof that evidence does not exist or that the issues are absent.

## Next verification sequence

1. Inspect the full INE IMC 2024 tables for water, labour and household conditions.
2. Read ADECO's underlying ICE calculation reports and document the basket assumptions.
3. Review the full World Bank 2026 economic update and 2025 climate report rather than relying on press summaries.
4. Triangulate the Brava report with transport/port/operator records, local price evidence and additional reporting.
5. Add dedicated sources for healthcare, housing, education, care, energy/waste and media before making stronger claims.
6. Seek voluntary, informed input from people with relevant local knowledge before designing an intervention. Do not imply community endorsement without it.

## Research ethics

Do not infer harm from religious belief, identity, high media use or group membership. Investigate specific practices and observable effects—such as coercion, deception, financial exploitation, isolation, interference with healthcare, or inability to refuse or exit—only where evidence supports doing so. Apply comparable standards across organisations and systems.
